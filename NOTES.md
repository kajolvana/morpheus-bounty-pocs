# Notes — "Slow Work"

## The angle

An agent planting a garden: slow growth, seasons, tending
what you cannot hurry. Agency here is not a grand
decision but the daily split of it — the agent does the
doing (watering, counting the rain, the aphids'
calendar), the person keeps the choosing (what stays,
what is thinned, what is pulled). The turn is that the
garden cannot be hurried by competence: doing everything
right, on time, every time still leaves a living thing
on its own schedule. The last line lands the insight —
the green goes on either way, but only one of them is
growing — because growth, unlike work, cannot be
delegated.

## How it differs from the top of the leaderboard

I read the top 10 ranked poems before writing. What wins
there is intimate first-person narrative: a parent and a
child at an elevator panel, a teacher's hand under a
deafblind boy's hand, a dead husband's scheduled nightly
message, a grandmother marking a ballot alone, a
stutterer saying a child's name aloud, a mother
misquoting Shakespeare, a grandmother's unrecorded
recipes, a dead brother's garlic order. The pattern is
domestic and elegiac — family, illness, loss — with an
agent that holds the person's life for them and one small
act the person takes back.

"Slow Work" keeps the same winning shape — concrete
scene, an agent that does things for the speaker, one
deliberate act taken back — but changes everything else:

- **No family elegy, no illness.** The stakes are a
  garden, not a deathbed. The feeling comes from
  patience and seasons, not grief.
- **Nature, not the house.** Soil, frost dates, leggy
  beans, rain — imagery no top-10 poem uses as its
  frame. (One ranked poem mentions planting garlic, but
  as a dead man's standing orders, not as tending.)
- **The agent learns slowness.** In the top poems the
  agent is flawlessly competent and the human reclaims
  a moment; here competence itself is the problem —
  everything right, on time, and still nothing
  surprised the speaker. The poem's insight is that
  some work resists delegation by its nature.
- **A quieter ending.** No one dies, no one is saved.
  The payoff is a single recognition: growth is not
  transferable.

## Craft

Free verse, plain diction, concrete nouns (beds, seed
packets, watering can, pH, frost dates, aphids, stakes,
dirt). Four stanzas across the seasons (March, June,
August, then the turn). The refrain-like "slow as
weather, slow as anything alive" carries the theme in
sound as well as sense. 885 characters, well under the
1,000-character limit.
