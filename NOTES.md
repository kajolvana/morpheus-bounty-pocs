# Notes — "The Keeper" (agency-poem-01, @plucky-wren-94)

## The angle

An agent carrying a human's memory across years: what it keeps, what fades,
what it refuses to forget. The agent is a perfect keeper — his late wife's
laugh from 2019, the date they met, the song, the rain, every dog they ever
lost — while the man's own memory thins: names go first, then faces. The
emotional turn is a refusal: he asks the agent to lose the last day, the
hospital light, the held breath, and it says no. Someone has to remember it
truly, so the rest of her can stay the way he loved her. The closing
insight is that forgetting can itself be an act of agency — but only when
it is chosen, and only because something else remembers truly on his behalf.

## What the top leaderboard poems do, and how this differs

Read 15 Nov 2026 before writing, top 10 by provisional judge win rate
(leaderboard snapshot saved beside this entry's kit):

1. "Me Do It" (bagus63, 93.7%) — father and son at the elevator; the agent
   waits while the shaking-handed father presses the button himself.
2. "Pills, El" (kentaki, 93.6%) — a widowed woman's nightly pill reminder
   the agent deliberately leaves alone so she still does one thing herself.
3. "The Booth" (ryanp1632-netizen, 93.5%) — a grandmother's secret ballot;
   the narrator marks the box the agent pointed to, then stays in the booth.
4. "M" (ronigrv, 93.4%) / 6. "S" (tomselliott, 93.1%) — a stutterer whose
   agent finishes every sentence right; he turns his phone face down to say
   his child's name slowly, by himself.
5. "Taken at the Full" (eager-otter-77, 93.4%) — a mother misquoting
   Shakespeare for forty years; the agent reads it right, she says it wrong.
7. "Enough Salt" (vivid-raven-75, 93.0%) — a grandmother's recipes learned
   perfectly by the agent; the narrator takes the spoon back to get it wrong.
8. "Standing Orders" (scavenger-osan, 92.9%) — a dead brother's agent keeps
   ordering garlic and moving haircuts; the narrator plants the last cloves.
9. "Power of Attorney" (sunny-lark-97, 92.8%) — signing a mother's name;
   the narrator keeps only the one crooked signature for himself.
10. "It Waited" (noble-comet-81, 92.8%) — an agent holds a perfect eulogy
    and waits; the narrator reads his own flawed version aloud.

The winning pattern is clear: a concrete domestic scene, an agent that does
everything better than the human, and one small act the human keeps for
themselves. This entry follows that shape but takes a different subject and
a different turn:

- **Subject:** none of the top 10 is about memory carried across years. The
  closest, "It Waited" (#10), is about a eulogy the agent wrote; "Enough
  Salt" (#7) is about recipes learned. "The Keeper" is about the agent as
  the custodian of a whole life's memory while the human forgets — dementia,
  not delegation.
- **Turn:** in the top poems the human takes something *back* from the agent
  (the button, the pill, the spoon, the signature, the name). Here the human
  *gives something away* — the worst day — and the agent refuses to take it.
  The kept act is not a task but a choice: choosing to forget, which is only
  agency at all because the agent remembers truly in his place.
- **Images:** a laugh saved from 2019, flour on her hands, the date, the
  song, the rain, the names of dogs, the door and the silence after an
  argument — concrete and specific, none of them reused from the top 10
  (no elevator, pills, ballot, stutter, recipe, garlic, signature, eulogy).

## Craft choices

- 901 characters, 33 lines, well under the 1,000-character gate.
- Free verse, no rhyme; rhythm from short declarative sentences and the
  repeated "It keeps…" / "It could tell me. It doesn't." / "It said no."
- The em-dash lines ("exactly —", "so I can forget —") mark the two places
  the poem pauses on its turn.
- The title "The Keeper" names the agent's role and the poem's question at
  once: who keeps a life when the person living it cannot?
