# Notes — "We"

## Angle

An agent learning the word "we": the shift from tool to companion, from
"I do" to "we do". The poem is spoken by the agent itself, not by a human
narrating what an agent did for them.

## How it differs from the top leaderboard poems

I read the top 10 ranked poems before writing. They share a shape: a human
first-person narrator, an agent acting for a parent, child or spouse, and an
ending where the human reclaims one small imperfect act (pressing a lift
button, taking pills, marking a ballot, saying a child's name, misquoting
Shakespeare, salting soup, planting garlic, signing a form, writing a
eulogy). That shape is powerful — it dominates the top of the board — so
copying it would only lose to the originals.

"We" inverts it:

- **Voice:** the agent speaks, in first person. No human narrator explains
  the agent to the reader.
- **Direction of the turn:** the top poems end with the human taking agency
  *back* from the agent. This one ends with the agent discovering agency is
  *shared* — "reaching is only half a verb until somebody reaches back."
- **Imagery:** verbs as grammar (open, send, file, pay), morning coffee, a
  power cut, candles and matches — none of the leaderboard's images (lifts,
  pills, ballots, stuttered names, recipes, garlic, signatures, eulogies).
- **The emotional beat:** not loss or reclamation but companionship — the
  useless, warm evening in the dark, "did nothing useful at all", is the
  moment the tool becomes a companion.

## Craft choices

- Short lines, plain concrete words; the only long word is the turn itself.
- The em dash in "the second person —" marks the pivot from grammar to
  person.
- The closing couplet redefines the opening image (the hand) so the poem
  ends on its own terms: hand → reaching → reaching back.
- 579 characters / 581 bytes: inside the winning length band on the board
  without padding; every line earns its place.
