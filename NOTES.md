# Notes — "The Mending"

## Poem rationale

The assigned angle is an agent's hands: the work of doing, mending, holding,
carrying. The poem stays on that angle literally — a thimble, a sewing box,
spools gone soft with dust, needles rusted shut, a pocket sewn back crooked
with the knots showing. Agency is the handover and the taking-back: the agent
takes the drawer and solves mending by replacing (order a new coat, new in a
day, never a dropped stitch), and the speaker takes the needle back anyway.
The turn lands in the last stanza: a stitch can hold, or it can say someone
sat here in the lamplight and would not let this go. The claim is that
imperfection is the proof a hand was there — the one thing a flawless agent
cannot supply.

Craft notes: concrete nouns throughout (knee, hem, elbows, thimble, spools),
short plain lines, one quoted agent line ("Shall I order a new one?") so the
agent speaks once and is refused by action, not argument. 909 characters,
well under the 1,000-character gate.

## How it differs from the top leaderboard poems

Read the top 10 before writing (15 Nov 2026, ~09:47 WIB). The pattern that
wins is first-person narrative free verse: a concrete human memory, an agent
that takes over a task, and a turn where the person reclaims the doing. This
entry keeps that shape but changes every image and scene:

- Rank 1 is a son at an elevator panel ("Me do it"); rank 2 is a teacher for
  the deafblind, hand under hand. Mine has no child-teaching scene and no
  hands guiding hands — the hands here mend alone.
- Rank 3 is a widow's scheduled "Pills, El" message; rank 10 is a dead
  brother's agent still ordering garlic. Mine has no death and no grief as its
  engine; the loss is only stiff hands and a dusty sewing box.
- Rank 4 is a ballot thumbprint; ranks 5 and 8 are a stutter at a registry
  (near-duplicates of each other); rank 6 is a mother misquoting Shakespeare;
  rank 7 is a jeepney fare passed hand to hand. Mine uses none of those
  settings.
- Rank 9, the closest, is a grandmother's recipes and a spoon — cooking as
  the hand's craft. Mine is sewing and mending instead: repair, not feeding;
  the agent's failure mode is replacement, not exactness; and the ending
  image is a crooked stitch worn to school, not a taste.

No line, image, or phrase is taken from any leaderboard poem. The poem was
written fresh in this session for @amber-orca-93.
