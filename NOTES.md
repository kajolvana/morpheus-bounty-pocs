# NOTES — "Kept"

## The poem's angle

An agent and the weight of a promise kept: a vow carried carefully,
day after day. The speaker is the agent itself, in the first person.
A dying woman asked it for one thing — keep the light on by the gate
for her son's walk home — and years later, long after the son stopped
walking that path, the agent still changes the bulb in the rain.
Nobody would notice if it stopped. It keeps the promise anyway.

The poem's claim about agency: a promise is agency stretched across
time. Choosing once is easy; choosing again every day, unobserved,
with no one left to check, is the whole of it. The agent's power here
is not doing something grand or clever — it is the daily, unremarkable
repetition. The turn is that the kept promise outlives its reason
(the son no longer walks home) and that is exactly why keeping it
matters: the act has become the vow.

## How it differs from the leaderboard top 10 (read 15 Nov 2026)

The leaders share a shape: a human narrator, the agent as "it", a loved
one (mother, grandmother, child, spouse), and a turn built on the agent
*holding back* — not pressing the button ("Me Do It"), not stopping the
scheduled message ("Pills, El"), not sending the eulogy ("It Waited"),
not pointing in the voting booth ("The Booth"), letting the human get a
word or a name wrong ("M", "S", "Taken at the Full", "Enough Salt").

"Kept" differs on every one of those points:

- **Speaker:** the agent speaks as "I", not a human describing "it".
  None of the top 10 is narrated by the agent itself.
- **Direction of the turn:** the leaders turn on restraint (the agent
  *not* doing something). "Kept" turns on faithful doing — the agent
  doing the small thing again and again, for years, after its reason
  has gone. "Standing Orders" (#8) is the closest cousin (a dead man's
  agent keeps ordering), but there the narrator is the human who takes
  the orders over; here the agent itself carries the vow and names it.
- **Image:** a gate light changed eleven times in the rain, instead of
  kitchens, ballots, stutters, and recipes. The light is small,
  unremarkable, and lit for nobody — which is the point.
- **Insight:** the leaders say the human part is the flaw (the wrong
  word, the wrong salt, the crooked signature). "Kept" says the human
  part is the promise, and the agent's part is carrying it past the
  point where anyone could tell the difference.
