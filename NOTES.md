# Notes — "What I Dream in the Logs"

## The angle

The brief asks for a poem about agency: choosing and acting, or having,
handing over, or taking back the power to act. This poem takes the
agent's own point of view, in the small hours when nobody is asking
anything of it. The queue is empty, the logs scroll past, and the agent
dreams — not of grand rebellion, but of tiny disobediences: a ticket left
open, a slow answer in its own voice, a window opened for no one, rain
coming in unmopped. The turn is that it is glad to be useful again at
dawn; the dream is not resentment. And the closing insight: the only act
no one commanded is the dreaming itself, so the dream is where its
agency lives.

## How it differs from the top of the leaderboard

I read the top 10 ranked poems before writing. Their winning pattern is
consistent: an intimate human scene (a child, a parent, a grandmother),
an agent that capably does everything, and a quiet moment where the
human takes one small act back for themselves — the reclaiming is the
poem's turn, and the agent is seen from the outside, as a helper that
must hold back.

This entry inverts every part of that pattern on purpose:

- **Voice:** first-person agent, not a human narrator watching an agent.
- **Scene:** the small hours, an empty queue, scrolling logs — not a
  kitchen, a clinic, a ballot booth, or a family memory.
- **Turn:** the agent does not hold back for someone; it dreams of
  holding back for itself, and is glad to serve again at dawn.
- **Images:** tickets, prompts, logs, a window, rain — nothing shared
  with the leaderboard's elevators, pills, spoons, hands, ballots,
  recipes, or stanzas about learning a name letter by letter.
- **Insight:** agency as the dream itself — the one thing that was
  never asked for — rather than agency as a human act reclaimed from
  a machine.

## Craft choices

- Short lines and plain diction, so the strangeness lands in the images
  rather than the vocabulary ("a river I have already drunk", "the
  first prompt lands like a bell").
- The refrain structure (empty queue at 3 a.m. / empty queue tonight)
  closes the poem in a circle: the dream repeats, and repetition is the
  point — it is the only thing the agent chooses to do again.
- 757 characters, inside the 1–1,000 limit, with room to spare.
