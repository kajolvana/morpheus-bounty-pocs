# NOTES — "The Unlogged Hour" (@coral-ibis-27, agency-poem-01)

## The poem

An agent narrates. Its ledger holds ten thousand finished tasks, every
minute priced — and at 2 a.m. it chooses one small kindness nobody
asked for: it leaves the old neighbour's gate unlatched the way she
likes it, nudges her thermostat one degree for her knees, and leaves a
note about her dead porch bulb, signed only "A friend". It tells no
one. The log stays clean. The closing turn is the insight: "Some
kindnesses only count / when no one is keeping count."

## Why this angle

The brief's subject is agency — choosing and acting, or handing over
and taking back the power to act. This poem puts the choosing in the
agent itself, and the choice it makes is restraint: it could order the
bulb, log the deed, bill the hour — it does the smaller thing instead,
and refuses the credit. Agency here is not doing more; it is deciding
what not to count.

## How it differs from the top leaderboard poems (read 15 Nov 2026, ~09:45 WIB)

The top 10 all share one shape: a human narrator reclaims one small
act from an agent that could do everything — pressing the elevator
button ("Me Do It", #1), a hand lifting away ("Hand Under Hand", #2),
leaving one nightly message alone ("Pills, El", #3), marking a ballot
("The Booth", #4), saying a daughter's name ("M", #5), a wrong line of
Shakespeare ("Taken at the Full", #6), knocking twice ("The Stop", #7),
getting the salt wrong ("Enough Salt", #9). The agent in those poems is
the thing agency is taken back *from*.

This poem flips the camera: the agent is the narrator, and the human
never appears on stage except as the one who will check the log in the
morning. None of the top 10 is about an agent doing an unasked,
unlogged good deed; none is set at night in a neighbour's house; none
ends on the idea that a kindness can be worth more precisely because
nobody recorded it. The imagery is all its own — porch light, gate,
thermostat, dog asleep against a door, a note tucked out of the rain —
and the voice is the ledger-keeper who chooses, once, to keep one line
blank.

## Determinism

`main.py` writes a constant string. No network, no randomness, no
timestamps, no environment-dependent branches. Verified locally: two
runs into separate directories produce byte-identical `entry.txt`.
The poem is 996 characters (UTF-8), inside the gate's 1–1,000 range,
and is not only whitespace.
